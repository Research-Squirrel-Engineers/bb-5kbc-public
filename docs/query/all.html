BB-5KBC: archaeological sites in Brandenburg, East Germany and West Poland around 5000 BC

Linked Open Data representation of some 540 archaeological sites in Brandenburg, East Germany and West Poland dating to around 5000 BC, with dating, cultural attribution, discovery events and georeferencing. The application ontology aligns with CIDOC CRM and its extensions CRMgeo, CRMsci and CRMarchaeo, with FSL for fuzzy statements and PROV-O for the provenance of the pipeline itself.

Every query on one page. One at a time, with its own address, is in the catalogue.

2. The cultural groups on a time axis

Where each cultural group sits in time, and how many sites carry it. The Mesolithic bar spans four thousand years and dwarfs everything else: that is the record, not a drawing error. Add FILTER(?st >= -5500) to see the Neolithic groups at a readable scale.

own page

3. What existed in a window of time

Sites whose dating interval overlaps the window set by the two sliders. Move either one and the query re-runs: -4800 to -4750 gives 349 sites, -4400 to -4300 gives 472, -4100 to -3900 gives 296. The test is overlap, not containment, so a site counts if it was in use at any point in the window — with intervals this wide that is the only honest reading. Widen the window to the full range and all 540 come back.

4. How the sites were found

The site type is known almost only where somebody excavated: stray finds and surface collections account for most of the record and leave the type unknown. That is a fact about research history rather than about prehistory, and it belongs beside every distribution map here. Group by ?type as well to see the cross-tabulation.

6. How well each site is located

7. Where the record hedges

Values the source marked with a question mark are not quietly cleaned up in this dataset: they carry fsl:certaintyDesc "uncertain". These are the site types the excavator would not commit to.

8. Sites per district, and how many are published

Where the corpus is dense and where the literature is thin. A district with many sites and few publications is not necessarily under-researched — it may be that its excavations are reported in a form the source database does not cite.

9. Where the coordinates came from

Every coordinate in this dataset was assigned by a georeferencing activity that records its own source. Provenance of the geometry, not of the site.

10. German and Polish sites per cultural group

The dataset straddles a modern border that means nothing to the fifth millennium BC. Grouping by country and culture shows which groups the border cuts through and which sit wholly on one side — a property of research traditions and of the source databases as much as of prehistory.
